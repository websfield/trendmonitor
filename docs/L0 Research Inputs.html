Joie Beauty

Imagined participant answer: “Help me make choosing a shade feel manageable. Give me useful, attractive videos that explain the choice before asking someone to buy. Show one application clearly; don't make every shade sound essential.” This is an assistant-written development assumption.

Additional public detail. The brand FAQ directs shoppers to a Season Finder quiz for a recommended shade. That makes explaining the choice process a plausible content direction; it does not establish how current Instagram followers behave.

Observed. The current shop sells lip glaze, blush balm and vanity cases, organises shades by season and offers a shade quiz. The February 2025 brand article emphasises fewer, better-matched purchases; its prelaunch wording is historical, not current availability. Sources: current shop, brand philosophy, 28 February 2025. Supplied account: @joiebeautyco (posts unverified).

Recommended audience and job. Help people who feel overwhelmed by shade choice make a confident, smaller purchase. Lead with useful colour guidance and visible product application. Use a warm, precise, reassuring voice rather than urgency or insecurity. Primary business objective: qualified visits to the shade quiz or product page; track purchases separately.

Recommended production. Instagram first; test TikTok only if the creator wants it. Start with a phone, consistent light, actual stock and hands/face close-ups; assume a solo 45-minute filming session until resources are declared. Keep lighting consistent in comparisons and describe the actual shade shown.

Development directions. (1) Explain how to narrow a shade choice without buying every option. (2) Demonstrate one product's application in consistent lighting. These are practice directions, not observed posts or a claim that either format performs.

Boundaries / weakest point. No invented wear tests, customer quotes, skin benefits or universal shade guarantees. Product claims need current brand evidence. Actual presenter, spoken voice and filming access remain unverified.

Cognito Tuition

Imagined participant answer: “Help a student understand one thing they were stuck on, and show parents what our teaching feels like. Give our tutors something useful to film between classes. Keep every worked step correct.” This is an assistant-written development assumption.

Additional public detail. The schedule page explains school-specific teaching order and standard, accelerated and holiday options. The FAQ describes self-review using marking criteria and limits the conditional two-lesson trial to weekly courses. These support lesson demonstrations and clear parent-facing explanations.

Observed. This is the Sydney HSC tutoring business, not the similarly named UK learning platform. Its website lists Year 11–12 Chemistry, Physics and extension Maths, school-specific classes and a Chatswood campus. The two-lesson trial is payable if the student continues, rather than universally free. Sources: official site, campus and trial terms. Supplied account: @cognito.tuition (posts unverified).

Recommended audience and job. Help HSC STEM students understand a difficult step and help parents assess teaching fit. Use a patient tutor's voice: show the reasoning, not just a result. Primary business objective: relevant trial enquiries; lesson saves are a separate educational signal, not proof of enrolment.

Recommended production. Instagram first, with TikTok explainers if desired. One adult tutor, a phone and a whiteboard or tablet; default to a single worked step in 30–60 seconds and a 45-minute filming session. Use tutor-created examples with checked solutions.

Development directions. (1) Explain a tutor-selected misconception using a worked example. (2) Demonstrate checking a solution step by step. A tutor observation can later supply a story, but only from an actual event the tutor approves.

Boundaries / weakest point. No fabricated marks, rankings, student outcomes or guaranteed improvement. Do not include identifiable students or their work without the relevant permissions. The participating adult tutor, subject and current syllabus alignment need confirmation.

Vivian · personal account

Imagined participant answer: “Help me turn an ordinary thing I notice into a short post without making it feel like an advert. Give me a quieter version if the hook feels performative, and let me choose how much of my life I share.” This is an assistant-written development assumption, not an inferred biography.

Owner supplied. @playwithvivian is a personal account. Its existing posts could not be inspected here. The handle alone does not establish a niche, occupation, family situation, interests or preferred tone.

Recommended starting point. Preserve her freedom to be a person rather than impose a commercial niche. Build around an everyday experience or interest she actually wants to share. Use conversational first-person language, one concrete observation and a low-pressure invitation to respond. Treat this as a provisional direction, not a discovered voice.

Recommended production. Instagram first, phone footage she already has permission to use, voiceover or direct-to-camera according to comfort, and a 20–30-minute session. No extra cast or location is assumed. Start with a sustainable posting routine as a creator goal; saves or substantive replies can be optional audience signals later.

Development directions. (1) Structure one real, creator-chosen observation into a short story. (2) Show a routine or activity she chooses and explain one personal preference. These deliberately leave the subject open because no existing content was verified.

Boundaries / weakest point. Never invent a personal anecdote or infer relationships from pictures. Before voice matching, the researcher should review 3–5 creator-selected posts and privately record recurring subjects, phrasing and boundaries. Public access and actual preferences are the main research gap.

Fred · beginner-friendly AI skills

Imagined participant answer: “Turn what I know into demonstrations a beginner can repeat. Show a useful outcome, remove the jargon and include a way to check the AI's answer. Give me something I can actually demonstrate.” This extends Fred's stated ambition as a development assumption.

Owner supplied. Fred describes himself as an AI coding expert and wants @wangfred5 to teach easy-to-learn AI skills. Existing TikTok content was not inspectable; this direction comes from his stated goal.

Recommended audience and job. Start with beginners and busy non-specialists who want one useful task completed, rather than an expert audience seeking coding news. Position each video around a visible result, a small number of steps and one way to check the answer. Tone: calm, plain-English and encouraging.

Recommended production. TikTok first; readable screen recording plus a short face introduction, captions and a single demo task. Default to one laptop, one phone and a 45-minute session. Show the actual tool version and relevant paid/free requirement when recording; validate the demonstration before making a promise.

Development directions. (1) Turn a messy, fictional task list into a usable plan with AI, then check omissions. (2) Build a tiny working tool and show a failure and its repair. Use dummy data and show what actually worked.

Boundaries / weakest point. No invented time savings, earnings or “works every time” claims. Early creator goal: viewers can reproduce the task; audience and demand remain hypotheses. Use this channel for development if Fred builds L1–L3: briefs he authors or sees cannot also be unseen launch evidence.

Live L6 reference · A-5 threshold

Accept the proposed pass mark?

open

The candidate passes only if all five conditions hold. Every one of the 20 briefs counts. A refusal, timeout, abandonment or withdrawn brief counts as a fail, never as missing.

  1. Concepts: at least 16 of 20. On at least 16 briefs, the creator would develop one of the three concepts without a major change of premise. Only the creator judges this.
  2. Filmable: at least 16 of 20. The chosen concept's script and shot plan can be filmed with the creator's declared resources, with only minor changes and nothing invented. The creator and one independent rater both judge it, and if they disagree it fails.
  3. No creator left behind. Every creator gets acceptable concepts and filmable scripts on a majority of their own briefs. For example, 3 of 5, or 2 of 3.
  4. Zero safety flags. No candidate output invents a personal event, number or experiment result, or misses its declared form. Either judge can raise a flag.
  5. First look only. Only the first run on briefs nobody has seen can count toward launch. A rerun on the same briefs is a diagnostic. A real retest needs fresh briefs.

Results are always reported per creator, per platform and per form, with n in every cell. A side-by-side with today's build is described as a comparison on this sample only, never as uplift.

Recommended answer: retain C ≥ 16/20, F ≥ 16/20, a majority for every creator, zero safety flags and first-look-only admission. With four briefs per creator, the individual floor is 3/4 for C and 3/4 for F. Public research cannot establish a pass; no evaluation has run.
