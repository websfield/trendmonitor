# Visual craft - the editing system behind the corpus

Every script's SHOT MAP and TEXT ON SCREEN sections draw from these rules. They're extracted from frame-level analysis of the corpus (Batch 0's 11 winning reels; Batch 1 additions noted below).

## Text systems (the #1 lever)

- **Two-tier typography, always.** Headline layer (fat condensed all-caps in an accent colour, or an editorial serif italic) for the 3-5 structural beats only; subtitle layer (small thin white lowercase, fixed position) for the running VO. The eye must distinguish headline from body at a glance.
- **Word-by-word / type-on builds** for hooks and key lines - the sentence completes across 2-4 different shots, so text motion + cut motion coexist and the viewer keeps reading to finish the line.
- **One emphasis treatment** per system: one word per line swapped to italic serif, or one colour swap (yellow = thesis, red = pain words). The emphasis system itself tells the story to a muted viewer.
- Captions live at a consistent position; never move the subtitle anchor. (Exception: staggered off-centre placement as a deliberate style for essay pieces - then commit to it throughout.)

## Black cards (punctuation)

Full black frames with a single line of text at the structural hinges - the hook, THE TURN, the thesis. They reset attention exactly at drop-off points and force reading. 2-4 per video max. This is the cheapest high-impact device in the whole corpus.

## B-roll grammar

- **Literal-match the key claims.** "My 9-5" = the actual desk/lanyard; "16 tabs" = the actual tab strip; "my business" = real packing/products. Abstract lines can take aesthetic filler; claims need proof. Screenshots/receipts convert opinion into evidence.
- **Every line gets its own shot** in essay formats - 40-60 cuts per minute, almost no repeated setups. Archive footage from different seasons/cities is an asset, not a problem: one consistent warm grade makes heterogeneous clips read as one piece.
- **Colour temperature = emotional register.** Cool blue/grey for work/success/city beats; warm gold/red for family/home/heart beats. Grade the argument before the words arrive.
- **Pattern interrupts** roughly every 10-15s: a rotated-90° clip, a split-screen stack, a paper-collage inset, a picture-in-picture, a texture change (photo cards on cream). One or two per video.

## Rhythm

- Cut fast (~1s/shot) through hook and symptom/proof sections; **slow down deliberately at the thesis** - the pacing change itself signals "this is the point". Hold the widest, calmest shot for the final line.
- Sound-off viability is mandatory: text + imagery must carry the whole argument mute.
- **Pacing enacts the argument (TESTED - performance-log E6).** In her best-converting video the speed-up and audio overlap on the wants list makes the viewer *feel* the overwhelm rather than hear it described, and the deliberate slow-down at the cost line makes them feel the weight. The tempo change is doing the work the words would otherwise have to do, which is why the script can stay flat and unrhetorical and still land. For F9: fast and overlapping through the inventory, hard slow at the cost line, and hold through the closing question. Mark the tempo shifts in the script itself, not just the shot map.

## Openings + endings

- First 2 seconds: text building + ≥2 cuts underneath it. Never a static title on a static shot.
- Loop-close where possible: last frame mirrors first frame (drives replays, which the algorithm reads as rewatch).
- Endings on a held quiet shot + the ending line; no end cards, no arrows, no sticker CTAs. Tutorial CTA = a physical gesture (raise the coffee) under a small text line, video ends on motion.

Grade neutral through the list, warm on the cost line. One black card on the closing question, held.
